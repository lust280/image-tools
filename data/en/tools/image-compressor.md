Big photos are slow to send and easy to reject on forms. This compressor re-encodes them in your browser and puts the original size next to the new size, with the percentage saved or added. PNG inputs are saved as JPG or WebP, because PNG has no JPEG-style quality slider.

## How to compress an image

1. Add one or more JPG, PNG, or WebP files.
2. Choose JPG or WebP and move the quality slider. Start at 75.
3. Compare the before and after size on each file, then download or save a ZIP.

## FAQ

### Do you upload the photo to compress it?

No. The before and after files are created in this tab. Nothing is posted to a server.

### What do the sizes mean?

“Before” is the file you picked. “After” is the file Foto just encoded. A negative percent means the new file is smaller. A plus means it grew, which can happen if the original was already heavily compressed.

### Why not compress a PNG as a PNG?

PNG compression is a different process and often barely changes screenshots. Re-encoding to JPG or WebP is what actually shrinks photos. Keep the PNG if you need transparency and sharp text.

### Is quality 75 safe?

For most phone photos, 75 looks clean on a screen and saves a lot of bytes. Use 85 or 90 when you are printing or cropping in close. Use [the resizer](tool:image-resizer) too if the pixel dimensions are larger than you need.
